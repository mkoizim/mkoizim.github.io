Anticipating Competitive Window Length

How long do MLB competitive windows last, judged by projections rather than results?

Projected cushion above the playoff line predicts whether a window stays open; window age tells a mixed story. Prior research measures competitive windows from results, but front offices have to make decisions based on predictions. Discrete-time logistic hazard models are fit using 2014–2026 Opening Day FanGraphs Playoff Odds under two window definitions: a team is in a window if it ranks within its league's postseason berths (berth-relative), or if it ranks in its league's top six (fixed). On the berth-relative definition, 56% of newly opened windows survived two seasons and 19% survived four. Each projected win of cushion above the first team below the threshold lowered the odds of a window closing (odds ratio 0.67; 0.72 at the fixed line) and also predicted actually missing the next postseason. Among teams with the same cushion, older windows were more likely to close on the berth-relative definition, but not on the fixed top-six threshold. Payroll and postseason format were not significant.

Exploratory analysis. Abstract submitted to the 2027 SABR Analytics Conference. Code written by an AI agent (Claude Code) under my direction; every decision and who made it is logged.

Player Cross-Effects

Do right-handed hitters protect the lefties around them?

Yes, through separation, and most clubs already collect most of it. Keeping left-handed hitters from batting back to back is worth up to about 5 runs a season, and it takes right-handed or switch hitters to do it. Teams already avoid most of that: reordering would add about 0.6 runs for the median 2025 club. The effect of any one neighbor on one lefty is 0.36 runs a season, about $0.4M, and not worth paying a premium for.

Regular seasons 2000–2025, 4.7 million plate appearances, Retrosheet play-by-play. Hypotheses and thresholds were fixed before the data were examined, and every table and figure rebuilds from public data with an automatic check. Coaching Compass

A prototype system helping coaches adapt to individual player learning preferences and development needs

Players answer a short survey on how they learn, how they like feedback and what motivates them. Coaches get a profile that combines those answers with performance stats and their own notes, plus an AI-generated summary with suggested dos and don'ts and the reasoning behind each.

First version built in under a day from plain-language prompts in an AI app builder (Firebase Studio), then cleaned up for public deployment. Next.js, Cloud Firestore and Google Gemini. All players in the demo are fictional.
