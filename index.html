Player Cross-Effects

Do right-handed hitters protect the lefties around them?

Yes, through separation, and most clubs already collect most of it. Keeping left-handed hitters from batting back to back is worth up to about 5 runs a season, and it takes right-handed or switch hitters to do it. Teams already avoid most of that: reordering would add about 0.6 runs for the median 2025 club. The effect of any one neighbor on one lefty is 0.36 runs a season, about $0.4M, and not worth paying a premium for.

Regular seasons 2000–2025, 4.7 million plate appearances, Retrosheet play-by-play. Hypotheses and thresholds were fixed before the data were examined, and every table and figure rebuilds from public data with an automatic check. Code written by an AI agent (Claude Code) under my direction; every decision and who made it is logged.

Coaching Compass

A prototype system helping coaches adapt to individual player learning preferences and development needs

Players answer a short survey on how they learn, how they like feedback and what motivates them. Coaches get a profile that combines those answers with performance stats and their own notes, plus an AI-generated summary with suggested dos and don'ts and the reasoning behind each.

First version built in under a day from plain-language prompts in an AI app builder (Firebase Studio), then cleaned up for public deployment. Next.js, Cloud Firestore and Google Gemini. All players in the demo are fictional.
